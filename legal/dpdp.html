DPDP Act & Data Protection for Schools

Children's data deserves the highest care. Compliance with India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023.

The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act changes how every school handles student, parent, and staff data. Here is what the DPDP Act for schools means in practice, exactly what NextGen School Alert commits to, what we already do, and our published roadmap to full compliance.

1. How We Look After School Data

We provide absolute security and operational transparency with our core commitments:

  • Stored in India: 100% of school production data, backups, and operational logs reside on secure Indian infrastructure (Supabase & Cloudflare).
  • Privacy by Design: Teacher and parent phone numbers are masked and hidden from each other by default.
  • Zero AI Training: School institutional data is never used to train external artificial intelligence models.
  • Zero Ads, Zero Adtech: No advertising networks or tracking ad SDKs exist anywhere within our ecosystem.
  • Your Data, Your School: Your school owns its records completely. We act strictly as an authorized data processor.

2. The Law in Plain Words: What the DPDP Act Asks of You

The DPDP Act, 2023 is India's premier comprehensive data protection legislation. Schools handle some of the most sensitive digital data in existence—children's data.

  • Scope: Covers all digital personal data (names, photos, phone numbers, marks, attendance, fee records, medical notes).
  • Parental Consent: For students under 18, schools must secure verifiable parental or guardian consent. Tracking or behavioral monitoring aimed at minors is strictly restricted.
  • Individual Rights: Parents and students can request access to stored data, request corrections, or request erasure within statutory timeframes.
  • Mandatory Security & Breach Reporting: Strict security safeguards (encryption, role-based access, access logging for 1 year) are required, and any security breach must be reported to the Data Protection Board without delay.

3. Our 8 Written Commitments to Every School

We are fully prepared to incorporate these 8 standing commitments into your institutional Data Processing Agreement (DPA):

01. Your Instructions Only

We process school records strictly on your documented instructions, never for our own commercial use.

02. Never Used to Train AI

Institutional data is never sent or used for external machine learning or AI model training.

03. Never Sold or Shared

We never sell, rent, or license school data to any third party for marketing or research purposes.

04. No Ads, No Adtech

Zero ad networks, zero ad tracking SDKs, and zero commercial monetization anywhere on the platform.

05. Stored in India

All active databases, system backups, and traffic logs remain securely on Indian cloud infrastructure.

06. Every School Separate

Multi-tenant architecture ensures strict data isolation; no school can access another institution's records.

07. Transparent Sub-processors

We maintain full transparency regarding infrastructure partners and provide advance notice for any updates.

08. Your Data Back, Then Deleted

If you exit our platform, you receive a full structured export, followed by verifiable permanent data deletion.

4. Division of Responsibilities (Roles)

Under the DPDP Act, compliance responsibilities are shared between the educational institution and the technology provider:

  • Your School (Data Fiduciary): Decides what data is collected, establishes the lawful purpose, provides privacy notices to parents, and handles stakeholder grievances.
  • NextGen School Alert (Data Processor): Stores and secures institutional data, executes processing tasks strictly on your instructions, and provides robust administrative tools.

5. Children First: Extra Care for Minor Data

Since the majority of school records belong to minors, the DPDP Act provides special protections. NextGen School Alert embeds child-safety principles directly into system architecture—ensuring strict parental consent workflows, hidden contact details, and absolute data segregation.
